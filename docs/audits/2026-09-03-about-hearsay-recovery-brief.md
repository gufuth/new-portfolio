## HEARSAY — current problem

## HEARSAY — current governing visual direction

- nearly roomless black space with one physical anchor
- actual supplied photos; do not recreate faces
- photos full color
- quote + person remain one unit
- copy large and readable
- tiny wall texture only
- subtle warm contamination
- faint pink continuity nod at left, not neon
- optional single hanging bulb/wire only if physically useful
- apparitions / ghosts in the dark, not framed objects on a wall
- multiple depth planes; perception shift rather than obvious element motion
- one strong foreground presence at a time, perhaps two maximum
- quieter people deeper in black
- no pile of small heads
- no constellation
- no busy overlap
- no magical flourishes just to make it strange
- attribution identity is the emphasis; do not let attribution disappear

## HEARSAY — interaction rule

The page does not need to be "interactive" to succeed.

Preferred behavior:
- all primary quotes remain accessible without waiting
- subtle drift/depth shift may enhance the apparition idea
- the viewer should feel perception changing, not watch elements perform animation
- no hover required to discover a testimonial
- reduced-motion version remains complete and visually intentional

## HEARSAY — production implementation model

Use a layered 2D stage, not generated compositing:
1. black / near-black environment plate
2. faint physical texture/contamination
3. real photo layers with masks/soft edge treatment
4. live HTML quote/attribution locked to each photo
5. one foreground group more present
6. deeper groups with restrained opacity/scale/blur only as needed
7. one optional physical anchor/light source
8. global navigation outside the apparition field

This keeps actual faces untouched and makes quote/photo pairs independently tunable.

## HEARSAY — acceptance test

At rest, within five seconds:
- user understands these are testimonials from real people
- at least one quote/person reads clearly
- page feels like Last Stop without becoming another diner booth
- there is no suggestion Ian wrote the quotes himself
- no face looks AI-generated or artificially reconstructed
- photo and quote read as one authored unit

The old framed-photo page stays `noindex` until this current implementation replaces it.
