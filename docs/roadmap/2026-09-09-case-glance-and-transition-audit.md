## Recruiter / hiring-glance rule

The first screen must prioritize hiring comprehension over authored flourish. Within a very short glance, a reviewer should be able to identify:

1. client / project;
2. what Ian owned;
3. assignment or central problem;
4. the governing idea;
5. one strongest verified proof signal.

Numbers should pop when they materially support scale, proof, longevity, object size, number of launches, number of criteria, or a similarly meaningful fact. They must not become KPI-card UI. The visual model is editorial interruption: large number, thin rules, quiet label, dark field.

No invented metrics. If a case lacks a meaningful verified number, use a qualitative proof signal instead of manufacturing one.

## Landing → Work cinematic direction

A still frame cannot approve this transition. It must be judged as a shot edit over time.

The previous implementation was too abstract: fade to black plus underexposed arrival. That is safe, but not yet authored enough.

World-class cinematography rule: do not animate the webpage. Motivate the cut from physical light and architectural occlusion in the scene.

Preferred sequence, roughly 550–700ms total:

1. Click / activation. The physical WORK sign gets a nearly imperceptible voltage acknowledgement, not a UI glow.
2. The exterior practicals dip by a fraction, as though exposure or electrical load changes.
3. A foreground architectural occluder / darkness crosses or consumes the frame rapidly. This provides the cut point.
4. At peak black, cut to the interior Work plate. Do not morph geometry between exterior and interior.
5. Interior arrives a touch underexposed and slightly warm/green contaminated from the exterior practical.
6. Eye adaptation settles over ~250–350ms. The contamination dies.
7. Rails remain stable so orientation never disappears.

The intended feeling is: `outside shot → physical interruption → next interior shot`, not `page A fades into page B`.

Sound, if enabled, may use one tiny motivated cue such as sign ballast hum / diner electrical tick / door or room-tone shift. No cinematic whoosh.

Reduced motion gets a direct cut with no choreography.
