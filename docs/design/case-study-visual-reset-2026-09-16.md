# Case-study visual reset — 2026-09-16

**Status:** current governing correction for case-study art direction. This supersedes case-study explorations that achieve Last Stop continuity by either (a) becoming a generic editorial/magazine microsite or (b) literally becoming another diner / Work environment.

## Governing law

**SAME VISUAL DNA DOES NOT MEAN SAME SET.**

A case-study page must look unmistakably related to Landing, Work 1, and Work 2, while remaining a work-first case-study page rather than another physical room, booth, diner window, roadside billboard scene, motel room, archive/evidence room, desk scene, or environmental set-piece.

The target is:

> The case study should feel authored by the same filmmaker and graphic sensibility as Last Stop, not photographed on the same set and not designed by a different magazine.

## Primary authority

1. Locked Landing is the primary visual North Star.
2. Work 1 / Work 2 are secondary proof of how Landing DNA can translate without becoming generic web design.
3. The current Drive case-study master contains the dated 2026-09-16 reset and remains the detailed case-study source of truth.

## Inherit from Last Stop

- restraint and quiet-before-beautiful judgment;
- darkness / negative space as compositional material;
- graphic flatness and asymmetrical stability;
- local color rather than a global neon or brown/green grade;
- dirty cream / imperfect black / brown-black / black-green framing territory where appropriate;
- material softness, low microcontrast, uneven resolution, restrained halation / grain logic;
- typography discipline rather than typographic spectacle;
- permanent film-frame / slate continuity where the site system calls for it;
- palette firewall: client work stays in native color;
- recruiter-first clarity.

## Do not inherit literally

- diner booth / window POV;
- roadside billboard staging;
- motel signage or room metaphors;
- another physical Last Stop set;
- environmental lore as the case-study container.

## Prohibited drift

Reject case directions that become:
- cream editorial microsites;
- fashion-magazine pages;
- giant-serif graphic exercises;
- KPI dashboards / stat rails / three-column proof modules;
- card grids;
- themed archive / ledger / evidence props;
- literal diner / billboard case-study scenes;
- generated substitute client work;
- text overlapping campaign images unless the source artwork itself contains it;
- rigid identical templates across every case.

## Recruiter hierarchy

A quick first glance should answer at least three of four:
1. What is this?
2. What was the governing problem / idea?
3. What did Ian own?
4. Why should I care / what proves it mattered?

Solve this through hierarchy and subtraction, not density.

Proof must be instantly understood. A naked number is not proof. Use a complete factual phrase, and only when sourced/verified. Never ship bracketed placeholder numbers from historical rewrite docs.
