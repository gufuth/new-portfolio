## Method

Five criteria, weighted by what the hiring research says recruiters actually screen for:

| Criterion | Weight | Why |
| :-- | :-: | :-- |
| Recruiter pull (brand/cultural gravity in the 3-second scan) | 25% | First-glance decision is real; big IP stops the scroll |
| Idea strength (named, ownable, retellable) | 25% | "Ability to ideate" is the top stated screen; named ideas survive interviews |
| Proof availability (awards, press, verifiable numbers) | 20% | Unsourced claims are a rejection trigger; awards and press are Tier-A proof |
| Gap-fill vs. Ian's current 8 (range/versatility) | 20% | Ian's book is social/digital-heavy, zero comedy campaigns, zero B2B, zero entertainment IP, one award |
| Role defensibility + copy visibility | 10% | The live-walkthrough drill kills overclaimed cases; only add what you can retell unscripted |

Already-overlapping brands (Virgin Galactic, 1800, Cuervo, The Atlantic, MoneyLion) are excluded as adds; they appear below as ASSET MINES for the pages you already have.

## The defensibility protocol (non-negotiable)

Every added case must pass three checks before it goes live, because the live unscripted walkthrough is how 2026 interviews test for borrowed and AI-inflated work: (1) you can retell the project for 90 seconds without the page open, including one messy production detail; (2) you can point at specific lines and say "mine"; (3) your resume timeline visibly covers the engagement (if a case came through freelance or a partner agency, add that line to the resume so the math checks). Shared work is normal: credit lines naming Zach as CD partner make you look senior, not smaller.
