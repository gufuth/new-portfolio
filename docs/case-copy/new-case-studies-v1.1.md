## 3. Selsun Blue — "Dan Driff"

# I wrote dialogue for dandruff. The MFA finally paid off.

**Selsun Blue · National TV + digital · [year] · [agency] · Copy, character development**

**The problem.** Nobody thinks about dandruff shampoo, which is the polite way of saying nobody thinks about Selsun Blue. The category runs on lab-coat claims and falling snow metaphors, and the brand needed people under 40 to remember it exists before they're standing in the aisle.

**The idea: Dan Driff, the world's worst wingman.** Dandruff, personified: the guy who shows up on your shoulder at the worst possible moment. The date, the interview, the dance floor. You can't argue with a flake, but you can wash him out of your life. Suddenly a claims category had a villain, and the villain was funny enough to share.

**The work.** Character development and scripts for the TV and digital spots: Dan's voice, his delusional confidence, the rule that he never knows he's the problem. Dan got exactly [three] spots' worth of rope, on purpose. Overexposed characters stop being funny and start being mascots.

## The hook register rule (encoded from operator feedback, 2026-07-06)

Hooks never argue the case's importance. They state the absurd fact flatly and let it sit. Three working shapes: flat statement + bureaucratic correction ("This is not a metaphor."); deadpan admission, self-placed small ("I read it twice."); self-deprecating true fact ("The MFA finally paid off."). Test: if the hook is selling, rewrite until it's just telling.
