# Portfolio Hiring Trends Deep Dive — 2025/2026

**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Companion to:** Portfolio Copy Audit v1 and Case Study Rewrites v1 **Method:** two off-bucket research runs (Perplexity sonar-pro) + web research. Sources at bottom.

## 1. What's table stakes now that wasn't in 2020

**A proper domain, built for the 3-second mobile scan.** Recruiters review on phones between meetings. A Behance grid or PDF book now reads junior for senior brand roles. Personal domain, fast, scannable, one screen per case. (Planet Group, book180)

**4–8 deep case studies, hard-curated.** The read threshold hasn't moved; patience has shrunk. Portfolio educators targeting CD-level books say 5–6 campaigns, lead with the strongest, end on the second-strongest. Fourteen tiles signals "can't edit," and editing is the job. (book180)

**Case studies structured as decisions, not galleries.** The anatomy reviewers scan for: context, problem, named idea, the work, results with sources. They're screening for "can this person simplify a problem and nail a brief," not for pretty screenshots. (HireQuotient, Copyfolio)

**Process and AI-workflow transparency.** This is the biggest shift. 2025-26 guidance tells senior candidates to show HOW work gets made: what you automated, what stayed human, how taste and brand integrity survived the tooling. "Presenting AI-generated work as entirely your own is a red flag that interviewers will catch" (AuthenticJobs). "Show your AI fluency: demonstrate how you use AI to automate routine work and free up time for higher-value thinking" (Verified Insider). In 2020 this page didn't exist. In 2026 its absence is noticed.

**LinkedIn as the parallel portfolio.** Headhunters increasingly start there. Visible POV (short campaign breakdowns, commentary on the craft) reads as leadership and communication skill. Your portfolio voice and your LinkedIn voice get compared; a mismatch is a flag. (Verified Insider, Tom Scott/LinkedIn)

**Video walkthroughs.** Short Loom-style case narrations are becoming standard for senior roles: they prove the story is yours, they show presentation skill (a core CD competency), and they survive the "walk me through it live" interview drill. Multiple assets ready: site, deck, Looms, LinkedIn.

## 2. How AI changed the screening itself

**Recruiters now actively screen for AI-written case copy.** The tells they name: repetitive formal "case study voice" with no specifics, identical phrasing across candidates, big claims that collapse under one probing question. The known lexical tells (negation-pair constructions, rhetorical pivots, adjective stacking) get books binned before a human finishes the first paragraph. For a copywriter this is existential: the book IS the writing sample.

**The live walkthrough is the anti-AI test.** Interviewers ask for unscripted retellings and drill into details AI can't invent: timelines, stakeholder politics, production constraints, what almost died and why. Case pages should be written so the live retelling matches: specific, messy in the right places, first-person honest about the team.

**Voice-matching.** Reviewers compare portfolio copy against your emails, LinkedIn posts, and how you talk. One consistent voice everywhere is now a trust signal.

**Implication:** the safest and most differentiating move in 2026 is a book so specific and so voiced that no one could mistake it for generated, PLUS an explicit page about how you actually use AI in your workflow. Deny nothing, hide nothing, show judgment.

## 3. What metrics reviewers expect, and how top books present them

- **2–4 numbers per case, not eleven.** A data dump reads as insecurity; heroes read as judgment.
- **Every number carries a source and timeframe.** "(client-reported, first 6 months)", "(platform analytics, May–Jul 2021)", "(StockX, 30 days post-drop)". Unsourced business numbers are a named rejection trigger.
- **Attribution language is the credibility unlock.** One line per case: what the results are, whose data, and your lane. It signals you understand multi-touch reality; claiming singular credit for brand-level outcomes signals ego or naiveté.
- **Creative-lane metrics are always safe** because you own them: hook/thumb-stop rates, engagement rate vs. category norms, completion rates, CTR, follower deltas, UGC volume, sell-through time, resale premiums, renewals. Business-lane metrics (revenue, share, subscriptions) need explicit attribution to the client's reporting.
- **Qualitative proof counts as results:** renewals ("the client re-upped four times"), adoption ("became the agency's standard framework"), press, awards, named-client testimonials.

## 4. What this means for ianrluna.com, in one list

1. Rewrite every case in one unmistakable voice (the site's jokes already prove it exists).
2. One screen per case: hook, context strip, problem, named idea, the lines you wrote, 2–4 sourced numbers, credit, takeaway.
3. Add the attribution sentence to every results block.
4. Add an AI-workflow page. You have a stronger true story here than nearly any working ACD.
5. Add 2–3 real testimonials beside the fake ones.
6. Record 60–90s walkthroughs of the top three cases when the copy locks.
7. Make LinkedIn sound like the site.
