# ABOUT copy audit

Status: review copy only. No route is approved for merge.

## Governing rule

The About copy must identify Ian quickly, establish real working range, preserve some dry personhood, and then stop. It cannot read like a positioning deck or a generic creative-leader bio. No unsupported years, titles, team sizes, awards, agency claims or invented client facts.

## Incumbent copy

> Ian Luna is a creative director and copywriter. His work has included Nike SB, Virgin Galactic, Porsche × Lucasfilm, MoneyLion × Beast Games, Outdoor Voices and The Atlantic.
>
> He works across campaign concepts, scripts, voice systems and the small lines that make the whole thing believable. He likes ideas that can survive a straight face.
>
> The room is paid through Thursday.

### Line audit

**`Ian Luna is a creative director and copywriter.`**

Factual and useful. It solves the basic recruiter question immediately. It is also impersonal and reads like metadata.

**Client list.**

Factual and useful based on the current site manifest. It proves range without adjectives. The issue is placement: folded into the first paragraph, it makes the paragraph read like a compressed bio rather than a person speaking.

**`He works across campaign concepts, scripts, voice systems and the small lines that make the whole thing believable.`**

Useful and relatively specific. Keeper territory. `The small lines...` has more character than the category list and should not be polished away.

**`He likes ideas that can survive a straight face.`**

Distinctive. Keep unless rendered rhythm proves it redundant.

**`The room is paid through Thursday.`**

Distinctive and site-specific without explaining the site. Strongest keeper.

## Historical Who material

Useful voice/evidence source:

- `Hi, I'm Ian.`
- writing / language background
- leather jacket / English Lit / third Squarespace / `Eat it, Dad.`
- long absurd rumor list
- scripts, manifestos, tone-of-voice systems, brand IDs, campaign concepts

Do not automatically import:

- bracketed ACD tenure or team-size claims
- bracketed AI outcome claims
- any historical sentence that depends on a stale employer state
- boilerplate creative-leader language

The old page's transferable quality is not length. It is the feeling that a person wrote it.

## Route A: Surgical continuity

> Hi, I'm Ian. I'm a creative director and copywriter.
>
> I work on campaign concepts, scripts, voice systems, and the small lines that make the larger idea believable. The work here includes Nike SB, Virgin Galactic, Porsche × Lucasfilm, MoneyLion × Beast Games, Outdoor Voices and The Atlantic.
>
> I like ideas that can survive a straight face.
>
> The room is paid through Thursday.

### Assessment

Best factual control. It converts the incumbent from third-person profile language into first-person speech without adding career claims. The client list remains proof, not self-description.

Risk: `the larger idea` is slightly more abstract than the incumbent `the whole thing`. Render both before lock; the incumbent wording may be better.

## Route B: Human signal

> Hi, I'm Ian. Copywriter first. Creative director by accumulation.
>
> I work on campaigns, scripts, tone-of-voice systems and the small lines nobody remembers assigning but everybody notices when they're wrong.
>
> Nike SB. Virgin Galactic. Porsche × Lucasfilm. MoneyLion × Beast Games. Outdoor Voices. The Atlantic.
>
> I like ideas that can survive a straight face.
>
> The room is paid through Thursday.

### Assessment

Most immediate personality and strongest scanning rhythm. The client line becomes a clean evidence beat rather than a clause buried in prose.

Risk: `Copywriter first. Creative director by accumulation.` is an authored characterization, not a fact already stated in the current About. It should not become shipping biography without Ian explicitly agreeing that it is true to how he wants to describe his career. `Nobody remembers assigning...` is voice rather than credential, but can feel written if the room does not make it effortless.

## Route C: Found evidence

Uses a tightened third-person factual bio close to the incumbent and leaves the voice turn to the final line / rumor discovery.

### Assessment

Safest if the room itself supplies enough character. It minimizes the chance that both image and prose try to perform at once.

Risk: third person restores the slight distance that made the incumbent feel like profile copy. The found-evidence interaction must earn that distance.

## Generic-language kill list

Do not introduce:

- multidisciplinary creative
- passionate storyteller
- where strategy meets creativity
- award-winning creative leader
- brands and culture
- ideas that move people
- creative problem solver
- pushing boundaries
- challenging the status quo
- indelible mark
- color outside the lines
- Swiss Army knife
- in the trenches
- any sentence whose meaning is mainly `I am creative and experienced`

## Current copy ranking

1. **Route A copy**: best merge-safe foundation because every substantive claim is already supported.
2. **Route B copy**: best voice experiment, but the lead needs Ian-level truth approval before it can outrank A.
3. **Route C copy**: useful control for testing whether the interaction can carry character without first-person prose.

No final copy should be selected in a text document alone. The winner is whichever version has the best line breaks, hierarchy, and emotional register inside the corrected room.
