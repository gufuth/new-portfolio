## Baseline verdict

The Room 3 premise survives. The hierarchy does not.

The current frame belongs broadly to the Last Stop film because the camera is ordinary, the materials are cheap, the room is mostly dark, the light is practical, and Ian is present indirectly through the CRT. The failure is not that the page needs a new concept. The failure is that the `LAST STOP MOTEL` sign behaves like the page's headline while Ian's biography behaves like supporting copy.

The correction should therefore remove emphasis before adding anything.

## First-glance order

Current desktop visual baseline:

1. `LAST STOP MOTEL` sign
2. Ian on the CRT
3. bed / room geometry
4. biography field

Desired functional order:

1. Ian / biography as one primary read
2. CRT as the human trace
3. room geography
4. motel sign as peripheral location evidence

The sign currently reads more like brand identity than geography. That violates both the current About instruction and the visual law that treats motel material as peripheral fact rather than logo lockup.

## KEEP

### Foot-of-bed camera

Keep. It is plain, temporary, and slightly indifferent. It does not look like a portfolio camera angle designed to impress.

### Room 3 premise

Keep. The room is ordinary enough to belong to the same night as Landing without copying the diner facade.

### Bed, chair, dresser and cheap materials

Keep. They provide physical truth without needing lore.

### Practical light logic

Keep. The room reads as lit by actual sources rather than by a global cinematic grade.

### Darkness and unused space

Keep. Darkness is doing compositional work, which is consistent with Landing. Do not fill the right side with more set dressing.

### CRT with Ian's real portrait

Keep as the page's indirect human signal. Do not generate, retouch, or substitute Ian's likeness. The CRT may support discovery, but it must not imply a fake reel or unfinished function.

### Stable FilmFrame rails and shared tour shell

Keep. They are the strongest system-level continuity device and already carry navigation, contact and sound behavior across the site.

### Live HTML biography

Keep the content as live semantic text. Do not bake copy into the plate.

### `The room is paid through Thursday.`

Keep as the default benchmark line. It is the cleanest collision of dry voice and environment in the incumbent.

## CHANGE

### Motel sign hierarchy

Change decisively. It is too bright, too legible and too memorable. Reduce it through physically plausible local exposure, saturation, focus, occlusion, crop, glass contamination or source-level sign failure. Do not solve it by adding a louder object.

Pass condition: in a blurred or grayscale thumbnail the sign is not the strongest luminance island, and the words need not read immediately.

### Biography hierarchy

Change. The current copy is honest but visually and verbally underpowered relative to the environment. Ian's identity and working range need a stronger first read without turning into a manifesto or resume.

### Biography integration

Change. The text currently feels placed into a convenient black area. Increase its compositional authority through scale, position, line length and a more intentional relationship with the CRT, not through a panel, card or glow.

### Human specificity

Change carefully. Recover some of the old page's personhood and dry self-mythology with fact-backed language and optional INCIDENTals discovery. Do not import old boilerplate or unsupported career claims.

### INCIDENTals

Change from absent to quietly discoverable. **INCIDENTALS is the locked label.** Preserve the original deadpan escalation as source material, but do not present it as `Rumors`, `Hearsay`, `About, allegedly`, `The rest is hearsay`, `Found in room 3`, or any new generic hidden-content label. Discovery may be enhanced by hover, but must also work by focus and touch. It should be reversible, keyboard reachable, assistive-technology legible and secondary to the biography. Do not add a new motel prop solely to justify it.

### Mobile composition

Change. The current breakpoint is usable but behaves like image followed by article. The goal is not to force a desktop scene onto a phone, but the phone version should preserve the human trace and room logic rather than reducing About to a banner image above copy.

## KILL

### Motel sign as hero / second logo

Kill. The sign is geography, not the About concept.

### `PAUSE II` as an implied feature

Kill as a functional idea. Do not give it reel, play, loading, coming-soon or interactive meaning. The phrase is baked into the current image, so source-level cleanup is preferable to a visible CSS patch. Until a clean source exists, do not pretend it has been removed.

### Fake reel / coming-soon behavior

Keep dead. The repo correctly removed it.

### Decorative CRT static loop

Keep dead. A constantly animated television would spend the page's motion budget to advertise an interaction.

### Old sound-on default, UTC clock and separate gold exit system

Keep dead. Shared tour behavior supersedes them.

### Generic creative-leader prose

Kill. No multidisciplinary-creative, passionate-storyteller, strategy-meets-creativity, brands-and-culture, ideas-that-move-people or equivalent language.

### New motel props or lore

Kill. Do not add key tags, room numbers, ledgers, telephones, ashtrays, postcards or other themed evidence unless a later route proves one is functionally necessary. The room already has enough physical truth.

### More glow, neon, fog, grain or atmosphere as repair

Kill. The hierarchy problem is subtractive.

## Rejected historical behavior

- Clickable CRT promising a bio reel: dishonest unfinished interaction.
- Sound defaulting on: superseded by shared sound-off preference.
- UTC clock / frame-number theater: interface lore with no About-page job.
- Longer placeholder bio containing unsupported years, role and client claims: cannot ship.
- Treating the motel sign as the proof of continuity: the visual law explicitly requires preservation of laws, not repetition of props.

## Current weirdness budget

The room already contains enough authored strangeness: Ian exists on a CRT in an otherwise empty cheap motel room, and the final line treats the room as if his biography happens to be staying there. Any INCIDENTals behavior must use that existing tension rather than introducing a second spectacle.

## Highest-leverage correction

Demote the motel sign before changing the concept. If Ian + bio can become the primary read while the same plate remains intact, Room 3 is worth preserving.
